Why it existed

Woot sold one item per day. During a Woot-Off, products could turn over in minutes—and getting the good one meant watching constantly.

This checker changed that. The page refreshed itself, repeatedly read Woot's inventory, and announced the moments that mattered: a sellout or the arrival of a new product. You no longer had to stare at the screen—or even remain in the same room—to know when it was time to come back.

It was the first checker of its kind to add an audio alert and was shared with the FatWallet community in June 2006. The first surviving changelog entry is version 0.1 on June 9. Later versions added different alert thresholds, public source code, custom search, a no-sound edition, a Kids.Woot tracker, and eventually Facebook's Like button.

A brief history

  1. Jun 2006

    The first audio alert

    Version 0.1 introduces the defining feature and the checker is posted to FatWallet.

  2. Jul 2006

    Captured on the early web

    The surviving Wayback Machine snapshot preserves the checker only weeks after launch.

  3. Nov 2006

    A very 2006 scaling problem

    Thousands of visits before 9 a.m. pushed a shared server's load above 70. DreamHost temporarily disabled the page; load dropped below 10.

  4. 2007

    More ways to listen

    Threshold-specific alerts, source release, and a no-sound version arrive.

  5. Aug 2011

    Version 1.7

    The Woot-Off tracker is repaired and a Facebook Like button joins the page.

  6. Apr 2013

    Last signal

    The final surviving status cache says the checker could no longer parse Woot.

  7. 2026

    Preserved

    The active tracker becomes a static exhibit. Its sounds and source artifacts remain intact.
